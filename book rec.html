My Top 3 Books

Song of Solomon

By Toni Morrison

Milkman Dead was born shortly after a neighborhood eccentric hurled himself off a rooftop in a vain attempt at flight. For the rest of his life he, too, will be trying to fly. As Morrison follows Milkman from his rustbelt city to the place of his family’s origins, she introduces an entire cast of strivers and seeresses, liars and assassins, the inhabitants of a fully realized Black world.

From: Storygraph

I read this earlier this year when I was experimenting with some English Literature syllabuses I found online, and I had read Toni Morrison's Jazz previously (back in 2019?) and I found it so pretty. I'd highly recommend this if you are interesting in reading about the black experience in America during the 1950s and before, especially following Milkman's story. Morrison tends to write in a non-linear way, but this novel is a lot easier to follow as Morrison tries to craft a linear storyline.

Things We Lost to the Water

By Eric Nguyen

When Huong arrives in New Orleans with her two young sons, she is jobless, homeless, and worried about her husband, Cong, who remains in Vietnam. As she and her boys begin to settle into life in America, she continues to send letters and tapes back to Cong, hopeful that they will be reunited and her children will grow up with a father.

But with time, Huong realizes she will never see her husband again. While she copes with this loss, her sons, Tuan and Binh grow up in their absent father's shadow, haunted by a man and a country trapped in their memory and imagination. As they push forward, the three adapt to life in America in different ways: Huong takes up with a Vietnamese car salesman who is also new in town; Tuan tries to connect with his heritage by joining a local Vietnamese gang; and Binh, now going by Ben, embraces his adopted homeland and his burgeoning sexuality. Their search for identity--as individuals and as a family--threatens to tear them apart. But then disaster strikes the city they now call home, and they must find a new way to come together and honor the ties that bind them.

From: Storygraph

While exploring a bookshop (as I often do when I get a chance), I found this book and picked it up. This was a few years ago, but I had finally read this in 2025 and whenever I think about it... It gives me psychic damage. My chest hurts. It was so incredibly sad but so hopeful, I would never read this out in public because I would be tearful from happiness or sadness. The characters were well written and the prose was beautiful, perhaps my only real criticism is how the narrative treats Hurricane Katrina, that only really shows up in the last 50 or so pages, so keep that in mind if you intend to read.

The Red Palace

By June Hur

Joseon (Korea), 1758. There are few options available to illegitimate daughters in the capital city, but through hard work and study, eighteen-year-old Hyeon has earned a position as a palace nurse. All she wants is to keep her head down, do a good job, and perhaps finally win her estranged father's approval.

But Hyeon is suddenly thrust into the dark and dangerous world of court politics when someone murders four women in a single night, and the prime suspect is Hyeon's closest friend and mentor. Determined to prove her beloved teacher's innocence, Hyeon launches her own secret investigation.

In her hunt for the truth, she encounters Eojin, a young police inspector also searching for the killer. When evidence begins to point to the Crown Prince himself as the murderer, Hyeon and Eojin must work together to search the darkest corners of the palace to uncover the deadly secrets behind the bloodshed.

From: Storygraph

An ex-friend of mine recommended this author, and while she is no longer in my life, the only thing I can be grateful for is this book! I study history, but I find that Korean history has evaded me for a long time, so getting a fictional narrative to serve the information was super helpful. This story is mostly a mystery, and a very interesting one, that follows a bold heroine and her ever-growing relationship with the police inspector that I didn't expect to like as much as I did. A really interesting story that follows some real historical figures, but shows the reality of how it was like being a woman in Joseon society.

Rapid Fire Favourite Books

Title Author Review
She Drives Me Crazy Kelly Quindlen An excellent romance that, in spite of the teen protagonists, they exhibit a maturity I find lacking in other romance novels.
Gingerbread Helen Oyeyemi A fantastical approach to the Black experience, with similarities to Toni Morrison but a writing style that Oyeyemi's characters shine in.
Democracy Joan Didion Evocative in all of its character, left me breathless through a lot of it, and a great approach to the political sphere post-Vietnam War.
Welcome to the Hyunam-dong Bookshop Hwang Bo-reum I wish I could say this is cozy, but instead it really made me feel large feelings for the characters, but I loved it all the same.
The House in the Cerulean Sea TJ Klune A wonderful little story that meets the mundane with the fantastical all the while you get to see a family of supernatural come together.
